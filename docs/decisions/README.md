# Earth decision records

Hot springs, lava and volcanoes: [0057](0057-geothermal-sites-and-volcanoes.md). Part 1 (geothermal rules 1) is **in Earth 2.0.0**, new worlds only (first in test build 1.0.3-tall.19): in new worlds the only hot springs are the 5,521 known ones (OpenStreetMap, ODbL, and GeoNames), each placed by vanilla's hot spring generator where the ground allows; the climate map's geologic activity is heat under the Smithsonian GVP's 1,214 Holocene volcanoes, capped at 128, instead of vanilla's noise, so deep cave lava comes only under them; vanilla's lava springs (lava out of mountain sides) are off. Part 2 (geothermal rules 2, volcano bodies), built at the author's request, is **in Earth 2.0.0** too (interiors since geothermal rules 3, [0078](0078-volcano-interiors.md)): the GVP's 825 volcanic mountains get a magma chamber in basalt and a conduit; the 332 active in the game's time (some 100–200 years after 1318: an eruption 1300–1700, or busy since) have open lava under a crust, a crater in their cones and fresh tephra around the vent; Erta Ale, Nyiragongo, Kīlauea, Erebus, Masaya, Villarrica, Ambrym and Nyamulagira have lava lakes; craters stay dry, without vanilla's ponds or springs. Eruptions later.

Scales, included GLO-90 and rivers on GLO-90: [0053](0053-scales-included-glo90-and-glo90-rivers.md) is **in Earth 2.0.0** (and every 2.0.0 release candidate); it was first built in test build 1.0.3-tall.5 (5 November, uncommitted; installed in the player's game 07:34); tall.7 (installed 20:07) joins full GLO-90's points by a smooth curve without noise in new worlds; tall.9 (installed 21:05) makes a random start take seconds instead of about 4 minutes on full GLO-90 by checking terrain only on the places it draws ([0037](0037-varied-random-starts.md), [0051](0051-glo90-elevation-on-demand.md) Verification). Earth size offers exact scales 1:400 to 1:10 (default 1:100), and World height, Mountain heights and Land heights default to Automatic for the scale. Four height data packs (153 MB) carry GLO-90 averaged to 24″ (about 741 m) as the new default land heights and the rivers laid onto GLO-90's valley floors; the detail noise (rules 1) is sized below the land data's cell; vanilla's tiled dungeons get the live map size. Checked on headless servers at 1:100 and 1:10 and in game on the AgentBridge bench. At 13 places: dry land 6–75 m from GLO-90 on the included heights (built-in 7–241 m); river windows cut more than 30 m below GLO-90 fall from 8–23 % to 0.2–2.4 % on full GLO-90. Test build tall.10 (5 November, late evening; built, not installed): full GLO-90 at every Earth size, from Earth's own data release on GitHub (`Temporal-Poets-Society/earth-glo90`, release `egl-v1`, 8.6 GB in five parts) with Copernicus as the fallback, the source chosen in the dialog or on the Earth tab (the download then starts when the world opens; [0051](0051-glo90-elevation-on-demand.md)); a block's height is GLO-90's mean over its footprint at 75 m and more per block; the Earth tab's rows grouped by their labels, with Mountain heights and Land heights off it; story locations searched on the included heights, which had stalled small worlds with the whole store. Test builds tall.11–13 (one play style per scale, Download all by default, exit channels judged on the built-in heights, lake outlets; tall.13 installed 6 November) and tall.14 (7 November; built, not installed): release/1.0's water atlas for new worlds only, its sea referee, river courses moved into the water mask and channel rules 4, shipped as difference files so older worlds keep the published data; and tall.15 (7 November; installed 13:38, tall.13 backed up; the tall line committed on `claude/tall-glo90`): the coastline is the water mask's wherever GSHHG's differs from it and the mask has that land too (sea referee 2), and rules 4's mouth erosion only in worlds without outline coasts (0053 items 11–15). Release/1.0 was merged into the tall line on 7 November at the author's request (`3fc1d7d`; its code was already there, and its rebuilt data files reach new worlds as the difference files), and test build tall.16 adds its river side arms on the rivers on GLO-90 for new worlds (0053 item 16; committed as `0266b74`, release/1.0 fast-forwarded to it; installed 14:20, tall.15 backed up). Test build tall.17 (7 November evening, combined with release/1.0's biome work, the December rain fix and ground columns) draws every lake of the water mask from its outlines in new worlds on full GLO-90 (0053 item 17; `lakes-glo90-1.bin`, 58 MB, travels with the GLO-90 heights; installed 18:35). Test build tall.18 (7 November night; checked on the bench, committed as `eabf485`, installed 20:08, tall.17 backed up) draws the rivers' water from the mask's outlines too, at the nearest course's level, with HydroLAKES reservoirs left out (0053 item 18; `rivers-outline-glo90-1.bin`, 30 MB, travels with the GLO-90 heights; [note](../research/river-outlines-2026-11-07.md)). Item 19 is a **proposal**, wired into new worlds for a test build (8 November): dams taken out of GLO-90 for new worlds on full GLO-90. Dam walls come down to the valley below them, river courses fall evenly to each dam's foot, a valley floor is rebuilt under each reservoir, and the river runs on through it as wide as it comes in (`dams-glo90-1.bin`, `DamRules` 105). Tall worlds: [0050](0050-tall-worlds.md) is **agreed** and **in Earth 2.0.0**. An Earth-tab World height slider (up to 1024) overrules vanilla's, which stops at 512, and an Earth-tab Rock floor makes the ground plain rock from 160 blocks below the surface in worlds taller than 512, so a 1024 world costs about what a 512 world does. With Sea level 160 and linear mountains this is a 1:10 Earth (Everest at y 1022). Headless server checked, not played. Ores checked: saltpeter stays underground; the floor removes sea-level ores under highlands, and prospecting readings leave out the floor since tall.3. Test build 1.0.3-tall.4 (installed 4 November) adds [0051](0051-glo90-elevation-on-demand.md)'s GLO-90 land heights (implemented, on `main` since 2.0.0-rc.1, the default for new worlds) and the river speed-up: dry land within 0.3–0.4 m of GLO-90 at 13 places, start-up 79–93 s → 14–16 s ([comparison](../research/terrain-vs-glo90-2026-11-04.md)).

Animals as of 1318: [0054](0054-animals-as-around-1318.md) is **agreed and in Earth 2.0.0** (not checked in game). Worlds placed as around 1318 give animals their range of about 1318: the natural range without what each species had lost by then or never reached after the Ice Age (no leopards or lions in Greece, no spotted hyenas outside Africa, no muskox in Eurasia, no dholes in America), never removing today's native range. Domestic animals went feral where people kept them (chickens across the Old World and Polynesia, not the Americas; goat breeds around their home areas; pigs into the Pacific). The game's tur is the East Caucasian tur.

Plants as of 1318 and one placement catalogue: [0049](0049-flora-ranges-as-of-1318.md) is **agreed** and **in Earth 2.0.0**. Natural plant generation follows each plant's range around 1318, native range plus introductions established by then, so there are no potatoes, maize or tomatoes in Europe and no olives in the Americas. [0047](0047-placement-catalogue-and-audit.md) is **agreed in direction**: one placement catalogue that does not depend on mods, the whole world including animals as around 1318, and a player setting back to vanilla climate placement. Its layers (per-provider mappings, per-path adapters) and audits (uncontrolled paths, in-game census) remain proposed. It was triggered by the olive report: vanilla's fruit-tree rule allows no olive anywhere in Greece ([research](../research/fruit-trees-2026-11-03/README.md)). A first slice is **in Earth 2.0.0**: fruit trees, wild crops and berry bushes by 1318 range and Earth climate, the **Plants and animals** world option, the placement inventory and `/earth census`.

Soil on semi-arid land: [0048](0048-soil-on-semi-arid-land.md) is **in Earth 2.0.0**. Vanilla's fertility formula, fed Earth's real rainfall, laid sand or gravel over 42% of Mediterranean land and half the steppes and dry savannas (sand on 24.7% of all land against 18.7% desert). New worlds now give every column with a real aridity index of 0.2 or more at least low soil, and deserts keep their sand ([research](../research/soil-2026-11-03/README.md)). Soil rules 2 (8 November, offline only) bring the same line to ground-column worlds, which had topsoil on all dry land: real deserts there have none, their cover's sand, silt or gravel showing, while river belts and semi-arid land keep it. Soil rules 3 (8 November, offline only) give arid floodplains their soil back: the natural flood of dry-season rivers, baked from Earth's rivers on GLO-90 (`floodplains.bin`), covers the Nile valley and delta, Mesopotamia and the Indus plain; dunes stay sand, and the Suez spot and the deserts stay bare.

Sea-level deposits: [0045](0045-sealevel-deposits.md). Vanilla's `disc-followsealevel` deposits were scaled for vanilla's sea level before Earth lowered it, so in tall worlds saltpeter coated the ground and coal, borax and sulfur sat in hills; Earth now records each generator's scale and rescales it at WorldReady. ## Status

Each record keeps these separate, because "agreed" is not "built" and "built" is not "checked":

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Proposed** | Written down, not agreed. Nothing may rely on it. |
| **Agreed** | The user decided it (date and words in the record), or it follows directly from an agreed rule. |
| **Implemented** | In the code, with the version and the files. Offline tests pass. |
| **Verified** | Checked in a running game, with the date and the world. |
| **Open** | A known problem whose fix still needs a decision. |
| **Superseded** | Replaced; the record names its successor and stays for history. |

A record moves forward by adding a dated line to its **History**, never by rewriting what was decided. Research reports
under `docs/research/` are evidence: records link to them, and they stay as they were written.

## Format

```
# NNNN: Title
Status: ... | Date: ... | Decided by: ...
## Context      what made a decision necessary
## Decision     what was chosen, precisely enough to check the code against it
## Alternatives what else was considered, and why not
## Consequences saves, performance, what it rules out
## Implementation  version, files, tests
## History      dated status changes
```
