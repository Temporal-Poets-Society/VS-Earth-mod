Hot springs, lava and volcanoes: [0057](0057-geothermal-sites-and-volcanoes.md). Part 1 is **implemented** on the tall line, in test build 1.0.3-tall.19 (`claude/tall-glo90`; checked offline and on the bench; installed, not played yet): in new worlds the only hot springs are the 5,521 known ones (OpenStreetMap, ODbL, and GeoNames), each placed by vanilla's hot spring generator where the ground allows; the climate map's geologic activity is heat under the Smithsonian GVP's 1,214 Holocene volcanoes, capped at 128, instead of vanilla's noise, so deep cave lava comes only under them; vanilla's lava springs (lava out of mountain sides) are off. Part 2 (geothermal rules 2, volcano bodies) is **implemented** on the tall line at the author's request (checked offline and on the bench). Both parts are merged into release/1.0 and installed as combined test build 1.0.3-tall.21 (8 November; not played yet): the GVP's 825 volcanic mountains get a magma chamber in basalt and a conduit; the 332 active in the game's time (some 100–200 years after 1318: an eruption 1300–1700, or busy since) have open lava under a crust, a crater in their cones and fresh tephra around the vent; Erta Ale, Nyiragongo, Kīlauea, Erebus, Masaya, Villarrica, Ambrym and Nyamulagira have lava lakes; craters stay dry, without vanilla's ponds or springs. Eruptions later.

Tall worlds: [0050](0050-tall-worlds.md) is **agreed** and **implemented** (not released). Animals as of 1318: [0054](0054-animals-as-around-1318.md) is **agreed and implemented** (`claude/fauna-1318`, not released, not checked in game). Worlds placed as around 1318 give animals their range of about 1318: the natural range without what each species had lost by then or never reached after the Ice Age (no leopards or lions in Greece, no spotted hyenas outside Africa, no muskox in Eurasia, no dholes in America), never removing today's native range. Domestic animals went feral where people kept them (chickens across the Old World and Polynesia, not the Americas; goat breeds around their home areas; pigs into the Pacific). The game's tur is the East Caucasian tur.

Plants as of 1318 and one placement catalogue: [0049](0049-flora-ranges-as-of-1318.md) is **agreed**. Natural plant generation follows each plant's range around 1318, native range plus introductions established by then, so there are no potatoes, maize or tomatoes in Europe and no olives in the Americas. It was triggered by the olive report: vanilla's fruit-tree rule allows no olive anywhere in Greece ([research](../research/fruit-trees-2026-11-03/README.md)). A first slice is **implemented** (not released): fruit trees, wild crops and berry bushes by 1318 range and Earth climate, the **Plants and animals** world option, the placement inventory and `/earth census`.

Soil on semi-arid land: [0048](0048-soil-on-semi-arid-land.md) is **implemented** (not released). Vanilla's fertility formula, fed Earth's real rainfall, laid sand or gravel over 42% of Mediterranean land and half the steppes and dry savannas (sand on 24.7% of all land against 18.7% desert). New worlds now give every column with a real aridity index of 0.2 or more at least low soil, and deserts keep their sand ([research](../research/soil-2026-11-03/README.md)). Soil rules 2 (8 November, offline only) bring the same line to ground-column worlds, which had topsoil on all dry land: real deserts there have none, their cover's sand, silt or gravel showing, while river belts and semi-arid land keep it. Soil rules 3 (8 November, offline only) give arid floodplains their soil back: the natural flood of dry-season rivers, baked from Earth's rivers on GLO-90 (`floodplains.bin`), covers the Nile valley and delta, Mesopotamia and the Indus plain; dunes stay sand, and the Suez spot and the deserts stay bare.

## Status

Each record keeps these separate, because "agreed" is not "built" and "built" is not "checked":

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Proposed** | Written down, not agreed. Nothing may rely on it. |
| **Agreed** | The user decided it (date and words in the record), or it follows directly from an agreed rule. |
| **Implemented** | In the code, with the version and the files. Offline tests pass. |
| **Verified** | Checked in a running game, with the date and the world. |
| **Open** | A known problem whose fix still needs a decision. |
| **Superseded** | Replaced; the record names its successor and stays for history. |

A record moves forward by adding a dated line to its **History**, never by rewriting what was decided. Research reports
under `docs/research/` are evidence: records link to them, and they stay as they were written.

## Format

```
# NNNN: Title
Status: ... | Date: ... | Decided by: ...
## Context      what made a decision necessary
## Decision     what was chosen, precisely enough to check the code against it
## Alternatives what else was considered, and why not
## Consequences saves, performance, what it rules out
## Implementation  version, files, tests
## History      dated status changes
```
