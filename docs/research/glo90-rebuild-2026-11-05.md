# Rebuilding GLO-90-like heights from about 200 MB (5 November 2026, evening; paused)

Status: experiment, not agreed, paused at the author's request ("maybe pause this. we need CPU for now").

The author's direction: ship about 200 MB that the player's PC turns into GLO-90-like heights once and saves to disk,
"to avoid large download"; "it maybe does not need to be full GLO-90 (8.6 GB) but much better res than we can cramp
currently inside 200MB". Today the included heights are a grid of cell means
([build note](glo90-coarse-packs-2026-11-05.md)); full GLO-90 is downloaded tile by tile (decision 0051, 8.6 GB stored
for the whole world).

The idea is a codec, not upscaling: an encoder that sees the real GLO-90 runs once on our side and spends the bytes
where the terrain needs them; only the decoder ships. Scripts and measurements:
[glo90-rebuild-2026-11-05](glo90-rebuild-2026-11-05).

## Classic codec first: JPEG 2000 at the grid's sizes

`baselines.py`: every 40th published GLO-90 tile (662) plus the 23 tiles of the 13 test places, read from
`rawdata/copernicus-glo90`. Per tile: the grid baselines simulated in Python (land-only means on 24″, 20″, 18″ cells,
sharpened 0.3 as the build, cubic spline back to the 3″ points) and JPEG 2000 (OpenJPEG through imagecodecs,
irreversible 9/7 wavelet, heights in 1 m steps) at 24 quality levels. The bytes are shared between tiles at one
rate–error slope (the cheapest total squared error for the bytes), as a codec built for this would do; worldwide size
= sample bytes × 26,475 / 662. Errors are over land points (GLO-90 ≠ 0).

- At the same size as tall.5's grid, an off-the-shelf wavelet codec is about 40 % closer to GLO-90 worldwide and about
  3 times closer in high mountains. The grid spends as many bytes on a plain as on the Himalaya; the codec doesn't.
- Flat land gets fewer bytes than with the grid (Gauja 6.7 m against 5.6 m); one slope for all tiles trades that for
  the mountains. A floor per tile would cost a little more.
- Not counted yet: the land mask at 90 m (sea is coded as 0 here; the mod's coast data or a coded mask would supply it),
  seams where tiles meet (each tile coded alone), a deterministic decoder in C# (OpenJPEG is native code), and how it
  looks: no close-ups yet.

## Learned codec: written, not trained

`model.py`, `train.py`: a small convolutional codec (four stride-2 layers, one latent per 16 × 16 points, GDN, a
factorized entropy model), trained on random land tiles outside the sample and the test tiles with loss = bits per point
+ β × land MSE. A 200-step smoke test ran (10 tiles). Paused before training; the plan is to train at a β that gives
about 200 MB worldwide, compare it with JPEG 2000 on the same sample and in close-ups at the 13 places, and time the
decoder per tile.
