# Full GLO-90 without noise: a smooth curve between the 90 m points (GLO-90 rules 3, 5 November 2026)

Status: implemented in test build 1.0.3-tall.7 on `claude/tall-glo90`, uncommitted; installed in the player's game 5 November 20:07. Prompted by the
author, 5 November evening: "I have been asking to fix full res GLO 90 data that we have at 8 GB to not have dumb noise
on it … Fix the full res first".

## What full GLO-90 did (rules 1 and 2)

On dry land a world on full GLO-90 (decision 0051) takes the downloaded 3″ heights and nothing else of the built-in
terrain: `TerrainModel.FineLand` replaces the column's height (`NaturalColumn`, `CoastColumn`). Between GLO-90's points
`FineElevation.TryHeight` joins them by straight lines (bilinear), and `FineLand` adds `FineUndulation` (1 m) of fBm
(56, 28 and 14 blocks) so the slopes aren't planes; rules 2 lifts a listed summit's own top to the survey. At 1:10
(about 10 m per block) a GLO-90 point is every 9 blocks, so slopes were flat facets with a crease at every grid line:
the contour terraces run in straight, grid-aligned segments.

The noise of rules 1 on the built-in or the included heights never reached full GLO-90's dry land. The author's
world "vintage adventure lands" (last played on 1.0.3-tall.3, 4 November) has no GLO-90 line in any of the player's
logs: it is on the built-in heights with detail noise rules 0, and keeps them (saved rules).

## Rules 3

`FineElevation.Version` 3, new worlds on full GLO-90 only (EarthParams.FineElevation 1 and 2 keep their rules):
- `FineElevation.TrySmoothHeight`: Catmull-Rom along each of the four nearest rows, each in its own column spacing
  (so across GLO-90's bands, where it thins its columns), reaching into the squares north, south, west and east at a
  tile's edge, then Catmull-Rom across the four rows. It passes through every GLO-90 point, as bilinear does.
- No undulation noise (`TerrainModel.FineGround`); the summit lift of rules 2 stays, measured on the same ground.

## Checks

`tests/ElevationTests` (100 sample tiles, the whole source as fourth argument): all pass, with new checks that a grid
point reads its own height, that the row interpolation is Catmull-Rom of its four points (7,280–7,521 m on Everest's
face), that the surface is continuous within 0.6 mm across 9° E, 47° N, the band edge at 50° N (1,200 | 800 columns),
60° N and the antimeridian, that Everest's GLO-90 top (8,726 m) reads back, that the slope changes by 0.001 m per
point across a grid line (6.0 m on straight lines), and that a fallback square gives no height.
`tests/EarthWorldOptionsTests` pass up to the known stop at the data-pack check (no pack zips in the worktree).
