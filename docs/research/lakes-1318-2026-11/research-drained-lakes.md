## 1. Key findings

1. **"Fill today's DEM to the old level" fails at most of the big drained lakes.** Ground has sunk: Mexico City more
   than 9 m in a century (Chalco's new lake stands about 12 m below the old ground), the Fens about 4 m at Holme Fen
   since 1851, Fucino's lowest point about 3 m since 1870, Copais about 4 m (W), Hula more than 3 m, Philippi
   more than 7 m. Hachirōgata's and Kolindsund's beds are below sea level. Ptolemaida is now an open-pit mine. The
   reverse also occurs: colmata (silt filling) raised Castiglione and Salpi. The workable recipe is **a traced outline
   plus a flat water surface at the old level, with the bed raised or carved to a plausible depth.** A plain fill
   works only where the bed has barely moved (Fucino's margins, Karla, the Turkish lakes).
2. **1318 is not "just before drainage".** Many 19th-century maps show Little Ice Age high stands. Examples: Fucino
   peaked in 1816 after a 1750–1861 rise and was very low in the 16th century; the Great Basin and California lakes
   (Tulare, Owens, Walker, Winnemucca) were near medieval low stands around 1300. Lake Cahuilla, today's Salton Sea
   basin, was most likely dry in 1318. The Aral was at its medieval low.
3. **Open, redistributable outlines exist for a few lakes.** OpenHistoricalMap (CC0) has pre-1635 polygons of all
   five Valley of Mexico lakes (846 km² together), Fucino (141 km²), Copais (328 km²), Hula (13 km²) and Antela
   (26 km²), but no source tags. CDMX's `lago1519` layer is a tracing of a CC BY-SA Wikimedia map, so a shape derived
   from it carries share-alike. For most other lakes the path is public-domain survey maps of the 18th–20th
   centuries (Austrian, British, US AMS, IGN Spain, Danish, Swedish and Swiss series, USGS topoView, USBR's 1905
   Klamath survey).
4. **Aral in 1318: about 29 m (range 27–30 m), not 31 or 35.**
   - Evidence: the medieval Syr Darya delta's edge north of Barsa-Kelmes lies at about 29 m; eastern-basin cores show
     lows of 26–30 m around 1150–1300; the Kerderi farms and mausolea stood dry at about 34 m.
   - Basins: the western basin held the lake; the eastern basin was a shallow lagoon. The Syr Darya crossed today's dry
     bed to it, and the Small Aral was low too, with no sign of a high North Aral spilling south.
   - Map: no open published outline exists. The 2006–2007 satellite Aral at about 29.5–30 m is a near-exact
     modern analogue.
   - Sarykamysh: probably a low lake (at most about 9 m, like today's 5–9 m) with the Uzboy dry, but this is debated.
5. **Bitter Lakes:** dry before 1869. The floor of the Great Bitter Lake held a layered salt bed 13 km × 5 km, up to
   13.2 m thick, with brine only in its grooves. The floor's exact depth below the sea was not found in an open
   source.
6. **New lakes found beyond the starting list:** Fetzara (Algeria), Greaca (Romania), Ciénega de Chapala and the Lerma
   lagoons (Mexico), Bakhtegan/Tashk (Iran, dried by dams), Mattamuskeet (USA), Sumas (Canada), Kolindsund and Filsø
   (Denmark), Les Moëres (France/Belgium), Federsee and Salziger See (Germany), Sassu (Sardinia), Ghab/Apamea (Syria),
   Emen and Gâvur (Turkey), Xynias (Greece), Ōnakanoko, Kahokugata and Shiunji-gata (Japan). Some of these are below
   10 km².
7. **Several places need water removed or kept dry, not filled.**
   - Mareotis (Alexandria) was largely dry in 1318.
   - Abu Qir lake did not exist until a storm breach of about 1770–84.
   - The Salton Sea dates from 1905–07.
   - The Lake of Homs was smaller before its 1930s raising.
   - Hongze grew mostly after 1194, in Ming and Qing times.
   - Padule di Fucecchio's lake came from a 15th-century dam.
   - Montady was drained in 1247–68.
   - Aydarkul (Uzbekistan) dates from 1969 (mem).

### A6. Notes

**Valley of Mexico.**
- **Area sources disagree.**
  - Frontiers 2023 (López-López et al., CC BY) built a 1,280 km² "Great Lake" in QGIS from today's HydroSHEDS DEM
    at a 2,250 m contour, plus Humboldt's 1811 map. This overestimates where the ground has sunk.
  - The OHM slices sum to 846 km² (pre-1635).
  - Humboldt's 1803 lakes were 428 km².
  - Figures of 1,500–2,000 km² for "pre-Hispanic" usually mean the high-water joined lake.
- **1318 specifics.**
  - Tenochtitlan was founded in 1325 (traditional date), so in 1318 there were no causeways, no Nezahualcoyotl dike
    (1449) and no Tenochtitlan island town.
  - Chinampas existed in Xochimilco–Chalco.
  - The lake level around 1318 is not known; floods in 1382 and 1449 suggest a rising 14th–15th c. trend (mem).
- **Recommendation.** Use the OHM polygons (CC0), checked against the Wikimedia/Niederberger map, with a flat
  surface:
  - Texcoco at about 2,237–2,240 m.
  - The southern and northern lakes 1–3 m higher, each held as its own level.
  - Raise the bed over the subsided city to 1–2 m below the water, because GLO-90 there is buildings over ground
    that has sunk about 10 m.
- **Rivers.** HydroRIVERS will send the basin's rivers out through the Gran Canal and the tunnels; in 1318 they end
  in the lakes.

**Fucino.** The best-sized and cleanest case.
- The probe's GLO-90 fill at 660 m gives 133 km², against a published 140–155 km² and the OHM polygon's 141 km². So a
  fill at about 660–662 m is a usable outline, with the bed in the old Bacinetto raised about 3 m.
- The 1318 level is uncertain. Giraudi reports very low levels in the 16th c. and a rise in 1750–1861 to the 19th-c.
  peak, so 1318 may well have been lower than the 660 m shore. A level of 655–660 m is defensible; note it as M–L.
- Close the Torlonia tunnel for HydroRIVERS. The lake is endorheic, draining only through sinkholes.

**Copais.**
- The 1311 battle in which the Catalan Company flooded the Frankish cavalry is often placed on the Kephissos near
  Copais (mem; its location is debated), which fits a wet basin in the early 14th c.
- The water level of 92–97 m comes from a hydrology model (Mamassis et al.). The probe's fill gives 112 km² below
  93 m and more than 195 km² below 95 m, consistent with 150–330 km².
- The ~4 m bed subsidence after the 1880s peat fires (W) means a fill at 95–96 m is too deep in the middle, but its
  edges are fine.

**Fens.**
- Only Whittlesey Mere is well sized. Its medieval extent was probably larger than the 1851 remnant: a 7.6–12 km²
  seasonal mere at the 17th–18th c. maps.
- Ugg, Ramsey, Benwick, Trundle and Brick meres: names and locations only (Saxton 1576, Speed 1610, Moore 1658–1720).
  Soham Mere: about 1,370 acres, undrained in the Middle Ages.
- Victoria County History, Huntingdonshire vol. 3, "The Middle Level of the Fens and its reclamation", should hold
  acreages; British History Online timed out during this session.
- A fill cannot draw them, because the fen has sunk about 4 m and GLO-90 shows Holme Fen's trees. Trace them from
  the PD maps (OS Old Series via NLS; Bodger 1786; Moore) and set water at about +1 m OD on a bed at about −0.5 m OD
  (estimate).

**Other cases with a 1318 warning:**
- Tulare, Owens, Winnemucca and Walker were near medieval low stands, and Cahuilla was probably dry.
- Dongting was in an expansion phase; its Four Outlets from the Yangtze are 1852/1870 breaches.
- The Katori inner sea was open to the Pacific.
- Comacchio's valli formed by subsidence from about the 10th c. (extent in 1318 unknown).
- The Moëres may already have been marsh.
- Balaton's higher medieval level is disputed.

**River re-routing (as important as the lakes):**

- Linth bypassing the Walensee (1811).
- Kander bypassing Lake Thun (1714).
- Agano mouth before the 1730–31 cut.

| Did water reach the Uzboy? | Probably not in 1318. Uzboy flow is archaeologically clear around 5–4 ka BP and in the 4th–5th c. AD (Igdy-kala). | **Syr Darya in 1318** | **Main flow to the Large Aral via the Kerderi braid** (active about 0.8–0.4 ka cal BP, about 1150–1550 AD): it forks near today's Oktyabr', where the river turns north, and runs west across the dry bed north of Barsa-Kelmes; a 120 m main channel lies 2 km south of Kerderi-1. **Recommendation for the mod (Part B).**
- **Aral.** Replace 0005's 53 m lake with a 29–30 m lake.
  - Western basin full to that level; eastern basin as shallow water or a lagoon at the same level.
  - Small Aral as a low separate lake at about 28–29 m. A small one is safer than a full one; nothing supports a
    full North Aral.
  - Syr Darya re-routed along the Kerderi braid into the eastern basin; Amu Darya split between Muynak-side western
    channels and the Daryalyk toward Sarykamysh.
  - Draw the outline from 2006–2007 imagery, or a 29.5 m GLO-90 fill checked against it.
- **Sarykamysh.** Keep a lake of today's size and level (about 5–9 m), dated "uncertain"; Uzboy dry.
- **Surroundings.** The 53 m-era coast, Kerderi's 34 m ground and the Puljai delta plain all stay dry land.
