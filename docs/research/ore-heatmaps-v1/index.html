What the colours mean

Evidence weight is an authored suitability score, not a measured probability or reserve estimate. The attempt view shows frequency × [background + (hotspot − background) × evidence] before native abundance, local texture, host, climate and depth checks. It is not the chance that each block contains ore.

Concentration controls the fraction of eligible deposit blocks filled. Quality bias changes poor/medium/rich/bountiful selection where the provider supplies those grades. Rich background deposits remain possible, but are less common. Surface starter deposits use their separate frequency setting.

Reported minimum burial only boosts attempts at the corresponding depth. Most records have no depth data; those records do not establish shallow accessibility. The default 5 metres per block is a configurable gameplay conversion, independent of the horizontal map scale.

Maximum combination avoids rewarding duplicate records. Survey-density normalisation lowers the weight of incidental records where the USGS MRDS database is very dense (mostly the United States) and widens the influence of lone records where surveys are sparse, but it cannot eliminate survey bias. Blank areas mean unknown; they do not mean geological absence. Mineral-specific hosts still come from the installed provider; a commodity map does not prove every mapped occurrence has that mineral form.

0.5° resolution and tiered point-influence radii (25–75 km) are deliberate gameplay generalisations. Static maps use a depth of 100 blocks. Tungsten and gypsum evidence is included, but the tested vanilla / Geology Additions combination has no usable block adapter for them.

Editing and save behaviour

The controls only change this preview. Downloaded JSON is a configuration fragment for earthmap.json; merge it with existing settings. Change source data and rebuild with tools/bake_ore_heatmaps.py to update geography. Existing saves retain settings and a data fingerprint. An explicit upgrade affects future generation; already generated chunks and cached ore maps are not rewritten.





Rock contacts across chunk boundaries
The catalogue is sampled through a coherent world-coordinate distortion. Neighbouring profiles meet in irregular lobes across a bounded transition zone; the full stratigraphic column remains coherent. A chunk boundary does not reset the pattern. Individual blocks still have one rock type.
