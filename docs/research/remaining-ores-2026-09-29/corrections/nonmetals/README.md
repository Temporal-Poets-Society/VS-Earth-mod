# Nonmetal and gemstone coverage audit

All 20 layers were checked. None is ready to define global absence or spawn probability.

Independent known-region checks. Countries are audit controls, never mineralization polygons. Production is used only as a contradiction check; geological resources and historical sources determine evidence type. A positive record somewhere in a country cannot pass a province-wide completeness test.

## Per-resource review

### B

**Check regions:** Mojave; Anatolian borate basins; Andean salars; Jarandol/Jadar. Borax/kernite, colemanite/ulexite and borate brines must remain separate. Sparse mines do not describe volcanic-lacustrine basin potential.

**Still needed:** Borate-bearing stratigraphic units and saline basin types; unworked deposits retained.

Sources: [source 1](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026.pdf#page=61)

### P

**Check regions:** North African/Middle Eastern sedimentary phosphorite belts; igneous apatite provinces. Large specialist inventory helps, but an apatite/carbonatite point cannot generate sedimentary phosphorite. Offshore resources are a separate accessibility class.

**Still needed:** Marine phosphorite formations versus igneous apatite hosts, depth and exposure.

Sources: [source 1](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026.pdf#page=147), [source 2](https://mrdata.usgs.gov/phosphate/)

### N

**Check regions:** Atacama nitrate fields; historically worked Appalachian/Kentucky cave sediments. Generic nitrate geography omits biological/cave and historical processing sources. Sodium and calcium nitrate evidence is not proof of naturally occurring potassium nitrate blocks.

**Still needed:** Nitrate-bearing desert units, cave sediment evidence and recipe distinctions; avoid nitrogen fertilizer production maps.

Sources: [source 1](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/1188/report.pdf), [source 2](https://www.nps.gov/places/saltpetre-vats-rotunda.htm)

### K

**Check regions:** Williston/Saskatchewan; Zechstein; Pripyat; Solikamsk; Khorat; Dead Sea. Permissive tracts are available but include brines and deeply buried strata. Modern mine closure does not remove potash geology.

**Still needed:** Separate solid sylvite/carnallite/polyhalite from brines, preserve kilometre-scale depths and nonproducing resources.

Sources: [source 1](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sir/2010/5090/s/), [source 2](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026.pdf#page=151)

### NaCl

**Check regions:** European evaporite basins and historical salt districts; continental salt basins. Existing potash supplement is not a global halite inventory. Sea-salt works and salt lakes are not underground halite evidence. Sparse known kernels cannot justify exclusion elsewhere.

**Still needed:** Mapped halite-bearing formations and buried evaporites, separately from brine and marine salt production.

Sources: [source 1](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026.pdf#page=165), [source 2](https://www.pgi.gov.pl/en/mineral-resources/maps), [source 3](https://www.nhm-wien.ac.at/hallstatt/en/salt_mine/beginnings)

### S

**Check regions:** Carpathian Foredeep native sulfur; sedimentary and volcanic systems. Composite sulfur layer is dominated by pyrite/sulfide feed. It cannot locate native sulfur; refinery recovery is another distinct source.

**Still needed:** Native sulfur-bearing carbonate/evaporite units and volcanic fumaroles; retain historical native sulfur deposits after production declines.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.pgi.gov.pl/en/mineral-resources/chemical-minerals/sulfur.html), [source 2](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026.pdf#page=187)

### C_graphite

**Check regions:** East African/Madagascan metamorphic belts; Sri Lankan veins; Norwegian graphite-bearing units. Mine-only or US-weighted points miss graphite-bearing metamorphic units and undeveloped resources. Flake, vein and amorphous graphite differ.

**Still needed:** Carbonaceous metamorphic formations, graphite form, inferred resources and depth.

Sources: [source 1](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026.pdf#page=97), [source 2](https://static.ngu.no/upload/Publikasjoner/Rapporter/2024/2024_023.pdf), [source 3](https://mrdata.usgs.gov/pp1802/)

### F

**Check regions:** Global fluorite vein and replacement districts, including closed/reopening European districts. Elemental fluorine in phosphate cannot automatically become fluorite. Existing record density badly overweights US coverage.

**Still needed:** Fluorite-specific vein, carbonate-replacement and intrusive provinces; historical districts and small occurrences.

Sources: [source 1](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026.pdf#page=85), [source 2](https://static.ngu.no/upload/Publikasjoner/Rapporter/2024/2024_023.pdf), [source 3](https://mrdata.usgs.gov/pp1802/)

### SiO2

**Check regions:** Quartz-bearing granites, veins, pegmatites, sandstones and metamorphic rocks worldwide. Ore-field/silica-mine points are fundamentally the wrong availability model for ordinary quartz. Gangue quartz and common rock-forming quartz were excluded; electronic-grade quartz has a much narrower distribution.

**Still needed:** Ordinary quartz abundance/vein model from geology, separate from industrial purity and gem quality.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.bgs.ac.uk/discovering-geology/rocks-and-minerals/), [source 2](https://www.ngu.no/en/geological-resources/fact-sheets-norways-mineral-resources), [source 3](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026.pdf#page=155)

### diamond

**Check regions:** Primary kimberlite/lamproite provinces and transported alluvial/coastal diamonds. MRDS alone misses many recorded districts. Legacy DIADATA adds historical/nonmined occurrences but includes imprecise gazetteer positions and stops around 2004.

**Still needed:** Primary host bodies versus source-linked placers, nonproducing occurrences and modern survey updates.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.prio.org/data/10), [source 2](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026.pdf#page=91)

### corundum

**Check regions:** Ruby/sapphire metamorphic and basalt-related districts and placers. Only 14 MRDS country labels and 90% US records. Industrial corundum, ruby and sapphire cannot share an unconditional gem-quality rule.

**Still needed:** Separate gem type, host and transported source; recent districts and legacy coordinate review.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.paivilujala.com/gemdata.html)

### emerald

**Check regions:** Colombian sediment-hosted and schist/ultramafic-associated emerald districts. Fifteen baseline records cannot represent global emerald geology. GEMDATA is a useful locality complement, not formation coverage.

**Still needed:** Specific beryllium-chromium/vanadium mineral systems and historical workings; ordinary beryl is not emerald.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.paivilujala.com/gemdata.html), [source 2](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2002/0110/)

### olivine

**Check regions:** Norwegian dunites and worldwide mafic/ultramafic olivine; peridot districts separately. Forty-five baseline records conflate industrial rock-forming olivine with peridot gemstones. Mine points understate widespread suitable lithology.

**Still needed:** Ultramafic/dunite units for ordinary olivine, distinct gem-quality peridot occurrence layer.

Sources: [source 1](https://static.ngu.no/upload/Publikasjoner/Rapporter/2024/2024_023.pdf), [source 2](https://webapps.bgs.ac.uk/bgsrcs/rcs_details.cfm?code=OLGB), [source 3](https://www.paivilujala.com/gemdata.html)

### garnet

**Check regions:** Metamorphic schists/gneisses, contact systems and heavy-mineral placers. US-biased industrial records and sparse gem records do not describe common garnet-bearing rocks. Gem species/grade must remain separate.

**Still needed:** Metamorphic mineral assemblages and placer provenance; per-species gemstone rules.

Sources: [source 1](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026.pdf#page=89), [source 2](https://www.paivilujala.com/gemdata.html)

### lapis

**Check regions:** Badakhshan; Chilean Andes; Baikal and Pamir lazurite districts. Two baseline records from one country are a severe coverage failure. Host association is specialized; blue rock or generic limestone is insufficient.

**Still needed:** Reviewed lazurite/lapis localities and contact/metamorphic carbonate systems; legacy positions need checking.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.paivilujala.com/gemdata.html), [source 2](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2002/0110/), [source 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.07409)

### alum

**Check regions:** Historically exploited alum shale and alunite systems. Five-country, 93% US mineral-mention map omits historical shale feedstocks. Alunite, natural alum salts and manufactured alum are different resources.

**Still needed:** Acid-sulfate alteration/alunite and suitable pyritic shale separately; historical processing supply must not generate natural alum crystals.

Sources: [source 1](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1018652), [source 2](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1018146)

### topaz

**Check regions:** Granitic pegmatite/greisen and rhyolitic systems; secondary placers. Thirty-seven baseline records in five countries miss broad known gemstone districts. Colour and recoverable crystal quality are not identified by ordinary topaz occurrence.

**Still needed:** Topaz-bearing intrusive/volcanic systems, weathered/placer pathways and historical locality review.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.paivilujala.com/gemdata.html), [source 2](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2002/0110/)

### tourmaline

**Check regions:** Pegmatite/metamorphic tourmaline districts worldwide. Ore-only 69-record baseline excludes widespread accessory/gangue tourmaline and many gem localities. Black schorl does not imply coloured elbaite gems.

**Still needed:** Separate common tourmaline from gem species and pegmatite zoning; include subeconomic and historical occurrences.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.paivilujala.com/gemdata.html), [source 2](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2002/0110/)

### beryl

**Check regions:** Beryl-bearing pegmatite provinces. Industrial beryl/beryllium, aquamarine and other colours cannot be inferred from one another. Existing points do not describe pegmatite province potential.

**Still needed:** Mineral-species and gem-variety evidence, pegmatite host/zoning and historical/subeconomic supply.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.paivilujala.com/gemdata.html), [source 2](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2002/0110/)

### spinel

**Check regions:** Gem spinel in metamorphic carbonate terrains and placers. Sixteen baseline records in five countries are far from global. Generic spinel-group minerals or ultramafic rocks do not establish gem spinel.

**Still needed:** Verified gem spinel districts, host reactions and placer sources; legacy coordinates require review.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.paivilujala.com/gemdata.html), [source 2](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2002/0110/)

## Data interpretation

The supplement imports 780 in-scope GEMDATA records and 1,174 DIADATA records, without requiring current mining or profitable extraction. The ambiguous MOGANITE field is withheld rather than silently classified as beryl. One DIADATA record has no resource identity and is withheld. Its duplicated original key TZ011DIA is retained as two distinct source features, with feature IDs appended. These legacy compilations have mixed gazetteer/locality precision and unreviewed runtime redistribution terms; they are research clues, not deposit masks.

The Afghanistan 2002 supplement keeps unambiguous, non-starred occurrence/deposit/field rows, including small and intermittent producers. Multiple-coordinate rows remain withheld. Publisher-restricted starred rows are excluded from derived geometry. Raw primary files remain local research inputs.

Geoscience Australia supplements retain both deposits and operating mines, source deposit styles and resource-size fields. Australian potash salt lakes are not sylvite/halite bedrock. Fluorine records without a mineral form are not automatically fluorite.

Depth, grade, early accessibility and economic status remain unknown unless reported. The revised atlas must not turn missing records or poor survey coverage into low geological grade. No game files are changed by this audit.
