# Baltic real geological columns: ground truth for the Earth underground layer pilot

Research date: 2026-11-06. Scope: Latvia first, then Estonia and Lithuania, with brief notes on the other neighbours. This is web research only: no code was changed and no large files were downloaded (PDFs were read as text in a scratch folder).

Conventions:
- Each fact carries a source tag `[Sn]`. The sources are listed at the end, with URLs and licence or reuse terms.
- **(inferred)** marks my own reasoning. **(unconfirmed)** marks something I saw only in a search snippet or could not open.
- Depths are metres below ground unless "a.s.l." (above sea level) or "b.s.l." (below sea level) is given.
- Latvian names: *svīta* = formation, *morēna* = till, *kvartārs* = Quaternary, *pamatklintājs* = crystalline basement.

**Check on the user's column ("thin dolomite, then rock-hard clay")** (inferred, from the formation lithologies in §2):
1. **Central Latvia (Riga, Ogre, Salaspils, Bauska).** The thin dolomite is the Pļaviņas–Salaspils–Daugava carbonate package, 22–50 m in total [S7]. The **Salaspils Fm** sits inside it and is clay, marl and clayey dolomite with gypsum [S3] [S7]. The Koknese Member at the base of the Pļaviņas Fm is dolomitic marl and clay [S3]. Below the carbonates come the Amata/Gauja sandstones with clay and siltstone interbeds. Further down is the **Narva Fm**, a 90–130 m clay, dolomitic marl and dolomite aquitard [S3] [S6] [S7]. Narva is the thickest "clay" in the Latvian column.
2. **NE Latvia.** Under a thin Pļaviņas dolomite come the Amata Fm (sandstone, reddish-brown siltstone and clay) and then the Gauja Regional Stage. In NE Latvia the upper part of the Gauja RS is the **Lode Fm**: clayey and silty deposits with lenses of very fine yellowish-grey clay (the Liepa clay pit) [S3]. **This confirms that the Lode clay is Devonian.** Note its age varies by chart: Middle Devonian (Givetian) in [S3], Upper Devonian `D3gj` in the LVĢMC reports [S7].
3. **Glacial till can also be "rock hard".** At Daugavpils the older Kurzeme (Saalian) till at the base of the Quaternary is described as dense, mostly hard ("blīvs, pārsvarā ciets") [S6]. At Ventspils the Kurzeme till is dense blue-grey [S5].

So the "hard clay" may be Devonian clay or marl (Salaspils, Koknese Member, Amata, Lode/Gauja, Narva) or an over-consolidated lower till, depending on the site. Only a borehole log can decide which for a given point (inferred).

- Late Weichselian deposits cover almost all of Latvia and dominate the sequence. Older units survive mainly in the uplands and buried valleys of E Latvia (Vidzeme, Alūksne, Latgale, Augšzeme) [S1].
- Glaciotectonic megablocks and thrusts are common [S1] [S4].
- About 95% of the Estonian Quaternary is glacial or aqueoglacial, with five till formations [S15].
- Glaciolacustrine basins in Latvian lowlands include the Zemgale, Venta, Burtnieks, Lubāns, Middle Gauja and Baltic Ice Lake areas, among others [S1, Fig. 3 legend].
- **(inferred)** The Zemgale plain is dominated by glaciolacustrine clay and silt over Latvian till. Kurzeme and Latgale uplands are dominated by till and glaciofluvial sand/gravel. I found no published "dominant sediment by region" statistic.

### 2.5 Estonia (brief)

- North Estonia: thin Quaternary over Ordovician and Silurian limestone, dolomite and marl. Under them lie Cambrian sandstone/siltstone and the Terreneuvian **Blue Clay** (Lontova Fm, >90 m in places), then Ediacaran (>120 m, max in NE Estonia), then basement [S14] [S3].
- South Estonia: Middle Devonian sandstone/siltstone (Narva clay/marl at the base); Upper Devonian dolomites only in the SE corner [S14] [S15].
- Ordovician up to 180 m in S Estonia; Silurian >400 m in total [S3] [S14].

### 2.6 Lithuania (brief)

- The same Devonian names continue south, with Lithuanian equivalents: Šventoji = Gauja + Amata, Kukliai/Butkūnai = Arukila/Burtnieki, Pamūšis = Ogre [S3].
- The Devonian reaches a 1000 m maximum near Gargždai (W Lithuania) and is absent in S Lithuania [S3].
- Permian, Triassic, Jurassic and Cretaceous (S Lithuania, up to 200 m) are present [S3].
- Silurian in SE Lithuania lies under 80–90 m of Quaternary [S3].

## 6. Caveats

- Several numbers come from hydrogeological reports that cite older Soviet-era and 1990s survey work (Juškevičs 1999, Levins 2006, Krutofals et al. 1992). They are good for typical values; they are not modern borehole statistics.
- The Ventspils table has internal inconsistencies as printed (the Quaternary 30–60 m range overlaps the Arukila top of 15–25 m; Ordovician 75 m vs regional 240–250 m). I report it as printed.
- Wikipedia "Geology of Latvia" figures (e.g. Devonian 900 m, Famennian 140 m) are mostly unsourced. They are used here only where marked.
- I did not find soil-depth or soil-profile sources (out of scope).
- NE Poland and W Russia/Belarus are not covered beyond the [S3] notes: Permian thickens to 300 m (Kaliningrad) and 600 m (Poland); Triassic to 400–500 m; Jurassic to 240 m; Cretaceous in S Lithuania. Each needs its own pass.
