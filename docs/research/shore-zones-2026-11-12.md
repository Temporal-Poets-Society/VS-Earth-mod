# Shore zones for plants: published widths (decision 0069 rule 7)

Web research, 12 November 2026, for the zones the author asked for ("Coastal zones and river banks"). **S**: a figure
stated in the source (abstract, excerpt or page). **I**: inference. Many full texts did not open, so several figures
come from abstracts or quoted excerpts; the sources are listed so they can be checked.

## Global beach data

- Luijendijk et al. 2018, "The State of the World's Beaches", Sci. Rep. 8:6641, doi:10.1038/s41598-018-24630-6:
  no beach widths. It classes shorelines sandy or not (31 % of the ice-free shoreline is sandy) and gives shoreline
  change 1984–2016 on transects every 500 m (24 % eroding faster than 0.5 m/yr, 28 % accreting, 48 % stable). The paper is
  CC BY 4.0; the data's hosting and licence were not confirmed.
- Lansu et al. 2024, Nat. Commun. 15:432: 235,469 transects; the median distance from a sandy shore to the nearest
  building or road is 392 m (space free of infrastructure, not a natural zone width).

## Rocky and cliff coasts

No widths in metres were found. The bare lichen and spray zone reaches higher on exposed shores; land plants come
further down on sheltered ones (MarLIN, "Yellow and grey lichens on supralittoral rock"). Mediterranean cliffs carry a
spray belt of Crithmum and Limonium (EUNIS RLB3.1b). Inference: sheltered Baltic, boreal and Mediterranean rock has a
bare band of 1–5 m, then trees; exposed Atlantic headlands grassland or heath for tens to hundreds of metres.

Scaling (I): bankfull width grows with about Q^0.5 (Leopold & Maddock; global widths in Andreadis et al. 2013, WRR
49:7164). Under about 5 m: trees at the edge; 5–30 m: a band of 1–5 m of grass, sedge and willow; large rivers: bare
bars inside the bankfull channel, then a willow belt, then forest; braided rivers: an active zone of 3–10 times the
low-flow channel. Riparian width grows with stream size, but large rivers with strongly varying flow can stay bare
(Muneepeerakul et al., WRR 2007, doi:10.1029/2007WR006100).

| No reed where the level varies by 3 m or more (S) | Ostendorp |

Rule (I): reed width ≈ (1.5 m of depth + about 0.5 m above the mean level) / tan(slope): about 140 m at 0.8°, 23 m at
5°, 5 m at 20°. The usual sequence beyond is sedge meadow, then willow or alder carr, then woodland (I; no widths).

## Arid regions

- Lower Tarim: leaf cover falls fast within 1 km of the channel and is near zero beyond (S); Euphrates poplar nearest
  the channel, tamarisk wider; groundwater 2–4 m deep suits them, 6 m is the limit (S; authors not verified).
- Tugai woodland: poplars where groundwater is shallow, oak and elm where it is deeper (Wikipedia, citing Thevs 2005).
- Savanna gallery forest, Benin: a continuous belt 10–30 m wide (Plant Biosystems 2012, doi:10.1080/11263504.2012.687404);
  the community changes 120 m from the river (Azihou et al. 2013, J. Trop. Ecol. 29:25); forest–savanna edges are abrupt.
- Inference: in drylands the pattern is inverted: trees only in a belt of tens of metres (savanna) to about 1 km (big
  desert rivers), set by groundwater depth, bare beyond.
