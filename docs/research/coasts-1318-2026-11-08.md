## 2. Where 1318 differs, worldwide (shown at 1:10; most also at 1:100)

**A. Sea or tidal flat in 1318, reclaimed since.** The Zuiderzee works (about 1,650 km², 1930–68) and Afsluitdijk
(1932); Wadden and Zeeland polders after 1318 and the Delta Works' dams; the Wash's marsh; the Po delta lobe; Venice's
fills (Marghera, Tronchetto); Tokyo, Osaka and Ise bays; Isahaya; Saemangeum, Sihwa, Incheon; China's coast (Bohai,
Tianjin, Caofeidian, Jiangsu, Shanghai's Nanhui, the Pearl River delta); Hong Kong, Macau; Singapore; Jakarta;
Manila Bay; Dubai, Bahrain, Qatar; Mumbai's joined islands; Lagos (Eko Atlantic); Monaco; Boston, San Francisco Bay.

**B. Lake or marsh in 1318, drained since.** Haarlemmermeer (pumped dry 1849–52) and the North Holland lakes (Beemster 1612,
Purmer 1622, Wormer 1626, Schermer 1635); the Valley of Mexico's lakes (drainage from 1607); Fucino; Copais; Karla
(1962); Giannitsa (1930s); Amik (1970s); Hula (1950s); the Pontine Marshes (1930s); Hachirōgata; Lammefjord; the Fens'
meres (Whittlesey Mere 1851); the Mesopotamian marshes (1990s); the northern Everglades; Kuttanad (reclaimed from
Vembanad, 19th–20th c.). Tulare, Owens and the Aral are done already (0005).

**C. Land in 1318, water today.** Canals: Suez, Kiel, Noordzeekanaal (1876), Nieuwe Waterweg (1872), Amsterdam–Rhine,
Manchester Ship Canal (1894), Volga–Don (1952), Karakum (1954–88), the St Lawrence Seaway, Grand Canal d'Alsace, the
South–North Water Transfer; harbours and docks. Lakes the mask has that are not reservoirs: opencast-mine lakes
(Lusatia, Leipzig, Rhineland), gravel and sand pits (Rhine, Po, Danube valleys), peat-digging lakes (Holland), salt
and aquaculture ponds (Bohai, the Gulf of Thailand, Camargue), accidental lakes (Aydar–Arnasay, Salton Sea, Toshka).

**D. Land in 1318, below the sea today by subsidence.** Holland's peat polders (peat shrank 1–4 m after drainage), the
Fens, the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta (to about −8 m), New Orleans, the Po bonifiche. In 1318 these were fen and bog
near or above sea level, not sea.

**E. Rivers and deltas moved by works after 1318 (river work, not a mask).** The Po (Porto Viro 1604), the Yellow River
(in 1318 on its southern course since the 1128 breach; today's course and Dongying delta since 1855), the Upper Rhine
(Tulla's straightening 1817–76), the Danube and Tisza cut-offs, Mississippi cut-offs.

**F. The reverse: works older than 1318 that Earth removes.** Item 19 drops every HydroLAKES reservoir it finds, so
pre-1318 tanks go too (Parakrama Samudra, Minneriya; Sri Lanka and South India have many: Veeranam, Bhojtal, Sayama-ike,
Proserpina and Angkor's West Baray all held water in 1318). Dates alone don't settle it: Mannō-ike (701) was a breached,
farmed basin from 1184 to 1628, so it should be dry. Item 19's own note: canals and reservoirs need "a dated list, not
a rule". The Yuan Grand Canal (1281–93, complete with the Tonghui in 1293) and Milan's Naviglio Grande existed in 1318
and should stay.

**Natural change after 1318** (the Grote Mandrenke 1362, the St Elizabeth's flood 1421, the Yellow River's 1855 shift)
stays as today's data has it, by the timeline rule (humans gone around 1318, natural events kept). Two cases where
1318 differs from Earth's chosen extent, for the author: the Aral stood low in the 14th century (settlements and the
Kerderi mausoleum lie on today's dry bed, about 60 km inside the 1960 shore; the Amu Darya returned to it from the late
16th century: [Kerderi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerderi)), while 0005 revives it at its 1960 level; and the
Bitter Lakes were a dry salt basin until the canal flooded them in 1869 ([Morcos 1967](https://oceanrep.geomar.de/id/eprint/55795/1/Morcos_S_1967.pdf)),
while 0020 keeps them as the grid's salt lake.

## 4. Proposal: "works after 1318" (new worlds only)

One sourced list, one bake, three outputs that match Earth's three water layers. Earth's own precedents:
`TERMINAL_LAKES` (0005, "man-made disasters are undone"), `CANAL_GAPS` (0020), `tools/fauna-1318.json` (0054), the
dam file (item 19).

1. **The list** (`tools/works-1318.json`): per entry a name, kind (`sea`, `lake`, `fen`, `land`, `keep`), the year of
   the work, the 1318 state, its outline source (an RCE 1250 class, a GeoJSON traced from a cited map, or a rule in a
   box such as "GLO-90 below −1 m reached from the sea"), a level for lakes, and citations. `keep` entries protect works
   of 1318 or before from the other steps (the Grand Canal, Bhojtal, the West Baray, medieval polders).
2. **Coasts:** a third stage after `tools/sea_ocean.py`, with the referee's own merge (`merge_outlines`,
   `update_terrain`, `sync_island_patches`). Two inputs: the list's `sea` entries (the Netherlands from the RCE 1250
   slice; elsewhere traced), which open GSHHG land to sea; and referee 2's "added" areas that Zhang's reclamation layer
   dates after 1318, which stay GSHHG's sea (the Palm, the airports, Tuas, Caofeidian, Nanhui), while natural accretion
   (the Meghna's chars) stays land. Written as `sea-referee.bin` version 3, so the grid flags, the ocean map, Sea and
   Ships see one coast. The polders' seabed is the grid's own (it already has them below 0 m). This changes the required
   Water Fix Data pack.
3. **Lakes and rivers:** `works-1318-1.bin`, keyed to `lakes-glo90-1.bin` and `rivers-outline-glo90-2.bin` by
   SHA-256 as the dam file is: lake and river pieces to drop (the list's canals; mask lakes that JRC Global Surface
   Water shows as new water since 1984, such as mine lakes) and 1318 lakes to add with their levels (the Haarlemmermeer
   group from the RCE slice, the Valley of Mexico from `lago1519`, Fucino, Copais, Hachirōgata), plus the reservoirs to
   keep, which go back into the lake and river outlines.
4. **Heights:** per-square patches in the dam file's layout: canal cuts filled across from their banks (the dam bake's
   smooth surface from the ground outside), dikes on restored water taken down to the bed, and subsided peat raised to
   a fen surface at sea level if the author wants fen rather than water.
5. **Runtime:** saved as `Works1318` (the next free EarthParams field, 106 on every branch today; older worlds 0). The file is read like the dam file (the GLO-90
   folder, else the data release, by size and SHA-256; a new world that can't get it stores `earth-works1318-off`). The
   lake layer skips and adds pieces, `FineElevation` applies the patch as it applies `Dams`. A lookup per column, as the
   dams (their probe: cost unchanged).

**Optional global rules, measured before adopting:**
- **R1, below the sea by the coast:** GLO-90 land below −1 m reached from the sea through ground no higher than 0 m,
  crossing at most two 90 m pixels of higher ground (dikes), becomes water at sea level where it is deeper than about
  2 m and fen at sea level where shallower. Natural depressions the sea doesn't reach (Caspian, Qattara, Dead Sea,
  Salton, Danakil, Turfan) stay. It catches polders worldwide without a list. It also floods medieval polders that
  existed in 1318 unless a `keep` entry protects them, and a natural barrier narrower than 180 m would be breached.
- **R2, sea dams:** the river bridges rule of item 19 applied to sea and lakes: a straight land line at most two
  pixels wide with open water on both sides is water (the Afsluitdijk, Saemangeum's wall, causeways). Natural spits are
  the risk.
- **R3, ponds:** the long tail (pits, salt and fish ponds, small canals) needs a classified source: OpenStreetMap
  (`water=canal|fishpond|salt_pond|reservoir|basin`, `landuse=quarry|aquaculture|salt_pond`) under ODbL, or GLWD v2's
  classes (already in `rawdata/glwd-v2`). Not proposed for the first pass.

## 5. Cost

- **Phase 1** (the list for about 30–40 entries: the Netherlands from the RCE 1250 slice, the big canals, the drained
  lakes above, the referee's added land sorted by Zhang's layer, the reservoirs to keep; bake; rules; bench): about
  three sessions, one each for research and tracing, bake, and code with tests and bench. Item 19 (dams) is the
  yardstick: prototype and global bake in one night.
- **Data:** `works-1318-1.bin` a few MB; `sea-referee.bin` v3 slightly larger than v2 (6.26 MB). A new Water Fix Data
  pack (required) and a new file on the data release, both needing the author's yes to publish.
- **Runtime:** nothing measurable (a per-column lookup, as the dams).
- **Downloads before building** (author's yes needed): the RCE 1250 slice (WFS, CC BY 4.0, small); Zhang's
  reclamation rasters (about 80 MB, CC BY 4.0); the Valley of Mexico `lago1519` layer (licence to check); JRC Global
  Surface Water tiles only where mask lakes need checking; Global Dam Watch 1.0 (CC BY 4.0) for the keep list.
- **Attribution:** RCE, Zhang, JRC and Global Dam Watch go into `overview/SOURCES.md` and Earth's credits.
- **Saves:** new worlds only; older worlds keep their coasts, lakes and heights.
- **Risks:** the polder floors are 3–6 m down, but `CoastColumn` makes any sea at least 6 blocks deep
  (`ShipSeaDepth`) a Reach (640 m at 1:10) from its coast, so the restored Zuiderzee would be a little deeper than it
  was: ship-deep, which suits Ships. Lake levels for drained lakes are estimates from sources. Phase 1 covers the
  listed places only.
