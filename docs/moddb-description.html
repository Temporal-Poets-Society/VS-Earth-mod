Version 2: rebuilt from the ground up

Explore the real Earth in Vintage Story.

Earth 2 is a complete rework with far more detailed real-world data, at scales from 1:200 all the way to 1:10, where one block is about 10 metres. Every mountain, valley and plain comes from the Copernicus GLO-90 land heights at 90 m detail. The real rivers run in their real channels, and lakes, beaches and coasts are traced from satellite and OpenStreetMap data. Every place has its real climate month by month, and plants and animals live where they lived around the year 1318.

Earth turns a new world into the real Earth: its land and sea floor, coasts, islands, rivers and lakes, the Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets, the real climate and weather of every place, and, if you choose, real rocks and ground. Sail from Riga to Lisbon, climb the Alps, cross the Sahara or winter in Siberia, and find each place where it is on a real map.

Earth 2 is one mod. Earth Terrain Data and Earth Water Data are no longer needed. Earth 2 makes new worlds: worlds from older Earth versions (0.x and 1.x) are not supported. From 2.0.0 on, saves are kept working.

What Earth needs

  • Vintage Story 1.22 (1.22.0 or newer).
  • Cylinder World 1.1.0 or newer: the world wraps east to west, the seam runs through the Bering Strait, and the poles are walls of ice.
  • Hydrology 0.6.2 or newer: calm rivers, rapids where rivers fall steeply, and water that stays where it belongs.
  • All three on the server and on every player's game.

What Earth downloads

The real Earth doesn't fit in a Mod DB download. The first time a server (or a single-player game) starts an Earth world, it downloads Earth's data once per computer from Earth's data releases on GitHub (Temporal-Poets-Society: earth-data, earth-ground, earth-glo90) into VintagestoryData/ModData/earth, and checks every file:

  • about 290 MB of map data, before the first world starts;
  • about 58 MB of monthly temperatures, at the first start of a world made with Earth 2.1;
  • the Copernicus GLO-90 land heights, 8.6 GB in all, with about 190 MB of lake, river and dam files beside them. By default all of them download in the background when a new world first opens, with a progress window; you can play meanwhile. You can also download them before the world starts, or square by square as the world reaches them.

A player joining a server downloads nothing beyond the mods: the server sends each player the monthly temperatures of the land around them. Without internet the files can be put in place by hand; a server that can't get them stops and says which are missing. Details: Data and performance.

Quick start

  1. Install Earth, Cylinder World and Hydrology (Sea recommended).
  2. Create a world with one of the Earth play styles: Earth · 1:200, Earth · 1:100 (a good first world), Earth · 1:50, Earth · 1:25 or Earth · 1:10. Each sets the world's size, height and mountains for its scale. At 1:100 one block is about 100 m; at 1:10 about 10 m.
  3. Open Customize for the Earth tab (rocks, ores, plants and animals, rivers, springs, weather); your start is Earth · Start on the Player spawn and death tab (a random place at your starting climate, one of 36 capitals, or coordinates pasted from Google Maps). The defaults are a good start.
  4. Play. Type /earth to see your latitude, longitude, real height and climate.

The world

  • Real geography at your scale: continents, ocean basins, continental shelves, coasts, fjords and islands. At 1:100 and finer the coasts follow the real coastline, and every island at least 4 blocks wide is there.
  • Copernicus GLO-90 land heights at every scale: every mountain range, valley and plain at its real shape, with surveyed summits restored.
  • The ring: walk far enough east and you come back from the west. The seam crossing is seamless, mostly by sea through the Bering Strait.
  • Greenland and Antarctica carry their real ice sheets.

Rivers, lakes and coasts as around 1318

  • The real rivers along their real channels, wide and deep enough for boats on the big ones. Calm rivers flow gently; white water only at the drops of steep rivers; the great waterfalls at their real heights. River mouths meet the sea in one bed.
  • Lakes at their real levels. The Aral Sea, Lake Chad and other lakes dried up by irrigation are back at their level before 1960.
  • Before the great human works: reservoirs behind modern dams are gone and the rivers run in their natural valleys; canals dug after 1318 (the Kiel, Corinth, Karakum and others) are land again; the Zuiderzee is back. Suez and Panama are land unless you turn on Suez and Panama canals, sea-level cuts for sailing.
  • Beaches of the coast's own material: sand, gravel or rock, from OpenStreetMap where it maps them.
  • Hot springs only where real ones are; volcanoes with magma chambers, active craters, eight lava lakes and fresh ash.

Climate and weather

Every place has its real climate, month by month: temperature and rainfall by latitude, altitude and season, opposite seasons in the two hemispheres, midnight sun and polar night. Earth's own weather follows it: wet and dry seasons, monsoons, prevailing winds, moving rain systems, thunderstorms and hail. Choose vanilla weather on the Earth tab if you prefer it.

  • Each place's own year (new in 2.1): every place's real mean temperature for each month, so Mumbai is hottest in May, before the monsoon, San Francisco in September, and the tropics swing only a little.
  • Day and night: each place's real day-night range for the month, 15 °C and more in a desert, a few degrees on a cloudy coast. The day follows the sun: coldest just after sunrise, warmest about two hours after noon.
  • Warm and cold spells as large as the place's real day-to-day swings at that time of year.
  • The climate as around 1318: early in the Little Ice Age, about 0.5 °C colder over the globe, 1 °C in western Europe, almost 2 °C in the Arctic. Snow, crops, vegetation and tree lines follow it.

Worlds made with Earth 2.0 keep their temperatures; UpgradeExistingWorldSeasons in earth.json gives one the new months, days and spells. Details: Climate and seasons.

Rocks, soil and ores

  • Rocks and ground: the game's own rock layers, laid under the real ground (the default), or the real rock of each region: ophiolites, granite districts, flood basalts, evaporites.
  • Soil by real aridity: the Mediterranean, the steppes and the dry savannas keep farmable soil; true deserts are sand and gravel.
  • Ores: vanilla's deposits plus Earth's gems (beryl, corundum, garnet, spinel, topaz, tourmaline) and ores the game doesn't place (chalcopyrite, tetrahedrite, azurite, cerussite, smithsonite and more, smelting to vanilla metals). Deposits lie at their depth below the real ground, so highlands keep iron and coal within reach.

Plants and animals as around 1318

  • Trees, fruit trees, wild crops and berry bushes grow where they grew around 1318: olives around the Mediterranean, no maize, potatoes or tomatoes outside the Americas, no wheat or grapes in the Americas.
  • Animals live within their ranges of 1318: wolves and bears across Europe, no lions in Greece. Farm animals have gone wild where people kept them. Chickens everywhere (on by default) gives every region feathers.
  • Biomes from the real climate: open prairies and steppes, treeless bogs, dry grass that greens after rain, alpine turf and scree. Or the game's own biomes.
  • Works with the Floral Zones packs, Wildcraft Trees and Fauna of the Stone Age, whose plants and animals follow their real ranges too. Include extinct animals lets mammoths and cave lions from those mods spawn.

Spawn and story locations

  • Start at a random place matching your starting climate (every part of the world gets a fair chance), at one of 36 capitals, or at coordinates pasted from Google Maps.
  • The game's story locations keep about vanilla's distances from your spawn, on suitable real land: never in the sea, on a glacier or on a mountain side.
  • Ruins, traders and translocators appear only where people lived around 1318.
  • With Astra Terra for Earth, the sun follows longitude as well as latitude, and a new world starts in the morning wherever you spawn.

Useful commands

  • /earth: latitude, longitude, real elevation and climate where you stand.
  • /earth trees, /earth plants, /earth geology, /earth ores, /earth fauna: the regional rules and data where you stand.
  • /earth tp <latitude> <longitude>: teleport to real coordinates (needs teleport permission).
  • .earthelevation and /earth elevation: the GLO-90 download's progress.

All commands: Commands on the wiki.

Reporting problems

Geographic data has finite resolution, some small features can't be shown at every scale, and optional mod combinations are still being tested. When you report a problem, give your Earth, Cylinder World, Hydrology and game versions, the world's scale and settings, the latitude and longitude (/earth shows them), your other mods, and attach server-main.log. Common problems and their fixes: Troubleshooting.

Shared roadmap

Earth and its connected mods have three main goals:

  • Educational geography: make real landscapes, climates, ecosystems and geological regions recognizable, and improve the geographic data and its representation at game scale.
  • Travel and exploration: make different regions worth visiting through their landscapes, resources and discoveries, supported by navigation, sailing and life aboard a ship.
  • Respect for Vintage Story lore: stay as close to the established lore as possible while fitting story locations to Earth's geography and developing a connected side story.

Planned content across the companion mods includes:

  • Earth Ruins: historically and geographically placed ruins of buildings that existed by the Diet of Kings in 1318, with architecture and ruin states reviewed against the lore and setting.
  • Underground Cities: explorable shelter cities, authored rooms, entrances, discoveries and regular translocators.
  • The Hansa voyage quest line: a campaign developed through Voyage and the ship, map and content mods, connecting discoveries, navigation, ship progression and journeys between distant places alongside the vanilla main story.

These are development targets for the connected mods. Earth provides the geography, climate and terrain services that support them; the complete linked campaign is still in development.
