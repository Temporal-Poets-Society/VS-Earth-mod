# Progression

What's easier or harder than in vanilla on Earth, and where to look for what you need.

With the defaults (vanilla rock layers and vanilla ores), the materials you need are where vanilla would put them, and
progression is vanilla's. What changes is the world around you: real climate, real distances, and plants and animals
by region. With ground columns or the ore maps, materials lie where the real geology puts them, and Earth adds recipes
so that every region can make tier-1 refractory bricks.

## The world itself

- **Climate is real.** Siberia is very cold in winter, the Sahara is hot and dry, the tropics have no winter but wet and
  dry seasons, and mountains are colder the higher you go. Where you start decides your first year more than anything
  else. See [Climate and seasons](Climate-and-seasons).
- **Distances are real.** The world is much bigger than a vanilla world: at 1:100 London to Paris is about 4,000
  blocks. Pick a scale that suits how far you want to travel. Rivers and coasts are the fast roads: boats on calm rivers
  and along the coast.
- **Seeds and animals are regional.** A wild crop grows only where it grew around 1318, so a European start finds spelt,
  rye, flax and turnips but no cassava, peanuts or sunflowers. With **Nature · Chickens everywhere** off, the Americas
  and Australia have no chickens to give feathers. See [Plants and animals](Plants-and-animals).
- **Soil in dry lands.** Semi-arid land, the Mediterranean and the steppes keep farmable soil instead of turning to sand.
  True deserts are sand and gravel.

## Flint

**Vanilla:** loose flint lies on the ground over flint deposits in sedimentary rock, and panning sand or gravel can
give flint.

**Earth:** with ground columns, flint lies loose where it really does: in the till, outwash, river gravel and beach
shingle of the lands the ice sheets covered, and chert weathers out of limestone almost everywhere. Panning sand and
gravel gives flint as in vanilla, everywhere.

## Refractory bricks without bauxite

**Vanilla:** refractory bricks, which you need for the coke oven and the cementation furnace, take crushed bauxite at
every tier. Vanilla places bauxite in many places.

**Earth:** with **ground columns** or **Earth ore maps**, bauxite lies only where it really forms. With ground columns
that is tropical laterite and the red terra rossa on warm limestone (a yearly mean of 12 °C or more); with the ore maps
on vanilla or regional rock, shallow lenses in the real bauxite districts. Most of the world has none. So those worlds
get extra recipes, with real historical substitutes:

| Recipe | Ingredients | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| Grog | Hammer + any fired brick | 2 grog |
| Fire clay | 8 blue or red clay + 1 grog | 8 fire clay |
| Fire clay | 8 blue or red clay + 1 crushed quartz | 8 fire clay |
| Refractory brick from fire clay and grog (tier 1) | 3 fire clay + 1 crushed quartz + 1 grog | 1 raw refractory brick (tier 1) |
| Crushed kyanite, sillimanite or andalusite | Hammer + the mineral | 1 crushed mineral |
| Refractory bricks, tiers 1–3 | Vanilla's recipes with crushed kyanite, sillimanite or andalusite, or the game's corundum ore, in bauxite's place | As vanilla |

- The **brick from fire clay and grog** is the medieval answer: plain clay tempered with grog (crushed fired brick). It
  costs more fire clay than vanilla's recipe, so bauxite stays the better choice where you have it.
- The crushed quartz still needs vanilla's pulverizer.
- **Kyanite, sillimanite and andalusite** (ground-column worlds) lie in metamorphic rock (schist, gneiss, quartzite,
  slate, phyllite), in the belts where that rock is: the Scottish Highlands, the Alps, the Scandinavian mountains, the
  Appalachians and the old continental shields. They need a copper pickaxe. Loose pieces on the surface show where a
  deposit is.
- **Corundum** here is the game's corundum ore, which only the ore maps place. Earth's rubies and sapphires don't count.
- Tiers 2 and 3 still need bauxite, one of those minerals or corundum, besides vanilla's other ingredients, so they may
  still mean a journey.
- Vanilla's own recipes, bauxite's included, work in every world.

In worlds with vanilla or regional rocks and vanilla ores, the extra recipes aren't there: bauxite is where vanilla puts
it.

## Volcanic-ash mortar

Fresh **tephra** (volcanic ash) lies around every active volcano on land, in every Earth world, and with ground columns
also on volcanic soils. In a barrel, 1 litre of slaked lime and 1 tephra make 6 mortar instead of vanilla's 4: the
pozzolana of Roman mortar.

## Fire clay from the ground

With ground columns, natural fire clay (kaolin) lies in the deep weathered saprolite of the tropics, also under
laterite. Vanilla's fire clay under coal seams is there as in any world.

## Ores

- **Vanilla ore deposits, plus Earth's gems and ores** (the default): vanilla's deposits, plus Earth's copper, lead,
  zinc and platinum ores, wolframite, pyrite and gems. See [Rocks, soil and ores](Rocks-soil-and-ores).
- **Earth ore maps (experimental):** ores are richest in their real districts; copper, tin, iron and coal also occur,
  more thinly, in suitable rock everywhere. This choice is being reworked.

## Where to look

- `/earth` and its sub-commands tell you about the ground, ores, trees and animals where you stand. See
  [Commands](Commands).
- A real atlas or Google Maps works: mountains, rivers and coasts are where they are on Earth.
