# Plants and animals

What grows and lives where. Earth's world is the Earth of around 1318: people have vanished, and the plants and animals
are those that lived in each place then, with the crops and livestock people had spread by that time, now gone wild.

## Two choices

**Nature · Plants and animals** on the Earth tab:
- **As around 1318** (default): trees, plants and animals grow and spawn only where they lived around 1318, and only
  where Earth's real climate suits them.
- **Vanilla, by climate:** the game, and any plant and animal mods, place them by climate alone, as in a vanilla world.

**Nature · Biomes** on the Earth tab:
- **Earth (by real climate)** (default): vegetation zones from the real climate set how dense forests and shrubs are.
  Fire keeps the prairies, the Pampas and the forest-steppe open; bogs and marshes stand without trees; dry land has less
  tall grass, which greens after rain and lies flat after winter; mountains get alpine turf, fell-field and scree.
- **Vanilla:** the game's own forest and shrub maps, fed by Earth's climate, and its tall grass.

The two choices are independent; every pair works. Neither changes terrain, rivers, coasts, soil or rocks.

## Trees and forests

**Vanilla:** each tree type grows wherever the temperature and rain suit it, anywhere in the world.

**Earth (as around 1318):** trees follow their real native regions (from botanical range data) as well as the climate:
a tree native to the Americas doesn't grow in Europe however well the climate suits it, and the other way round. Where
none of the installed trees is native to a region, the nearest fitting stand-in grows, so no forest is left bare.
Vanilla's trees alone are enough; with the Floral Zones packs or Wildcraft Trees and Herbarium installed, their trees
join in by their own native regions.

With **Biomes: Earth (by real climate)**, forests are where the real climate keeps them: the taiga and the rainforests
are wooded, the steppes, savannas and deserts are open, and trees stop at the real tree line on mountains and in the
Arctic.

## Fruit trees, crops and berries

**Vanilla:** fruit trees, wild crops and berry bushes grow by climate. On Earth's real climate that put olives in the
savannas of Africa and India and none in Greece.

**Earth (as around 1318):** each grows within its range as it stood around 1318: its native range plus the orchards and
fields people had spread by then. Later introductions are left out, above all the exchange between the Old World and the
Americas after 1492.
- **Olives** across the Mediterranean; **pomegranates** from the Mediterranean through south-west Asia to India and
  China; **apples, pears, peaches and cherries** where Europe, west Asia and China grew them; **oranges** only from
  India to south China.
- **Crops from the Americas stay in the Americas:** no wild sunflowers, peanuts, cassava, pineapples or grain amaranth in
  the Old World (nor, with Floral Zones, maize, beans, sweet potato or cotton).
- **Old World crops stay out of the Americas:** no spelt, rye, flax, rice, soybeans, turnips, carrots, parsnips or
  onions there, and no olives, oranges, apples, pears, peaches or cherries.

What it means for you: a European start finds spelt, rye, flax and turnips, but no cassava, peanuts or sunflowers. To
grow those you'll have to travel.

Ground plants from the Floral Zones packs grow only in their native ranges. Vanilla's own flowers and cacti follow 1318
ranges too (all but croton and rafflesia); vanilla's other ground plants still grow by climate.

## Animals

**Vanilla:** each animal spawns wherever the temperature and terrain suit it, anywhere in the world.

**Earth (as around 1318):** animals live within their ranges as they were around 1318: where they would live without
people, less the places people had already emptied by 1318 and places they never reached. So there are wolves across
Europe and bears across most of it (not Britain or Ireland), but no moose in Britain; with Fauna of the Stone Age, lynx
across Europe and no lions or leopards in Greece. Animals that live in a place today always count as living there in
1318.

- **Farm animals have gone wild** where people kept them in 1318: chickens across the Old World and the settled Pacific,
  sheep wherever sheep were herded, goats around their home regions (Valais goats in Europe, Angora goats from Anatolia
  to East Asia, Nubian goats in Africa and Arabia, Sirohi goats in South and South-East Asia), pigs, and fallow deer
  around the Mediterranean and in western Europe.
- **None of the game's land animals in Antarctica.** With Fauna of the Stone Age, penguins live on its coasts.
- **Chickens everywhere** (**Nature · Chickens everywhere**, on by default): wild chickens can also spawn wherever the
  climate suits them, so every region has feathers for arrows. Turn it off and chickens live only where people kept
  them in 1318 (not in the Americas or Australia). It only matters with Plants and animals as around 1318.

### Animal mods

With Plants and animals as around 1318 and **Fauna of the Stone Age** installed, the animals of its 21 packs that Earth
knows (Moschidae's musk deer among them) spawn only within their real ranges and habitats too; an animal from a pack Earth doesn't list keeps its own
rules. Animals still alive in 1318, such as the aurochs and the moa, spawn where they lived. **Nature · Include extinct
animals** (off by default) also lets species that had died out before 1318 spawn, such as mammoths, cave lions and
woolly rhinos. It adds no animals by itself: it needs a mod that has them. With Plants and animals: Vanilla, those mods'
animals spawn by their own rules, whatever the switch says.

## In every Earth world

Whatever the Nature choices:
- **Fish** live in rivers as well as lakes.
- **Coral** grows only in warm seas, not in warm lakes or salt lakes.

## Checking where you are

The `/earth` commands for trees, plants, placement and animals tell you what grows and lives where you stand, and
why. See [Commands](Commands).
