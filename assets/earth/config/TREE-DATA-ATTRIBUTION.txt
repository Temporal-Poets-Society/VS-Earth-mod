Earth regional trees, rules version 2 (2026-10-02)

Native distributions and accepted botanical names:
Govaerts R (ed.). 2026. WCVP: World Checklist of Vascular Plants.
Facilitated by the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew. https://doi.org/10.34885/egs6-cp24
Snapshot: 2026-06-04, downloaded 2026-09-29 from
https://sftp.kew.org/pub/data-repositories/WCVP/wcvp.zip
License: Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/).
Derived subset: accepted taxa and their native botanical countries; introduced
and doubtful distribution records excluded. Regionally extinct native records retained.
Changes: native records that are evident introductions or errors are removed and
listed with reasons in the source repository (tools/tree-taxa.json "exclusions";
currently silver birch, Betula pendula, in Argentina South).
No occurrence coordinates or present-day forestry plantations are used.

Botanical boundaries:
Biodiversity Information Standards (TDWG), World Geographical Scheme for Recording
Plant Distributions, 2nd edition (Brummitt, R.K. 2001); boundaries from
https://github.com/tdwg/wgsrpd (geojson/level3.geojson; originally ArcView shapefiles
provided by the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew; GIS dataset by Justin Moat).
LICENSING IS AN OPEN QUESTION. Open content on TDWG's website, including the WGSRPD
standard page, is licensed CC BY 4.0, but the boundary files themselves carry no
explicit licence: the github.com/tdwg/wgsrpd repository states none, and the file
metadata (level3/master_meta_data.html) gives use constraints "None" while preserving
the credit "copyright 1992-1997 ESRI, GMi, All Rights Reserved" for the base geography.
Earth does not claim a licence for these boundaries. The simplified polygons
(treeregions.json) are distributed pending clarification with TDWG / Royal Botanic
Gardens, Kew, and may have to be replaced by boundaries under a known open licence.
Changes: topology-preserving simplification at 0.015 degrees, coordinate rounding
to five decimals, conversion to compact polygon rings and a runtime spatial index.

Finer natural ranges (2026-11-07):
Caudullo, G., Welk, E., San-Miguel-Ayanz, J., 2017. Chorological maps for the main
European woody species. Data in Brief 12, 662-666. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2017.05.007
Data: Mendeley Data hr5h2hcgg4 version 19 (last update 20 Dec 2024).
License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).
Changes: native range polygons and isolated-population points (introduced and
naturalised layers left out) rasterised to a 0.1 degree grid from 20 N to 80 N, widened
by one cell, and combined with the WCVP botanical countries: inside a country where a
mapped species is native, its tree grows only within that species' map (regionaltrees.json
"rangeMask", "rangeSource"). Walnut, Italian cypress, bay laurel and white poplar, which
people had spread well beyond these maps by 1318, keep their botanical countries alone,
and so does yew, which the longbow-stave trade cut out of much of its range after 1318. Scots
pine also holds the Scottish Highlands (56.3-58.7 N, 5.9-2.6 W), where the mapped native
stands are the remnants of the Caledonian pinewood.

Soil indicators (2026-11-07):
Dengler, J., Jansen, F., Chusova, O., et al. 2023. Ecological Indicator Values for Europe (EIVE) 1.0.
Vegetation Classification and Survey 4: 7-29. https://doi.org/10.3897/VCS.98324
Data: supplementary material 8 (EIVE_Paper_1.0_SM_08.xlsx).
License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).
Changes: niche position and width (nw3) for moisture, nutrients and soil reaction of the
species each tree rule stands for, combined over its taxa weighted by the number of
indicator systems EIVE used (regionaltrees.json "eive").

Climate envelopes:
Per-species Earth climate envelopes (real yearly mean temperature, rainfall) are
derived from the WCVP native botanical countries combined with Earth's own baked
climate (earthdata/, see earthdata/ATTRIBUTION.txt for its sources).

Earth's interpretation:
Generic vanilla/Wildcraft forms use explicit regional botanical analogues (e.g. the
generic vanilla fir stands for regional firs and spruces); "regional analogue" rules
designate stand-in species for a biome where no installed native species qualifies;
cultivars are excluded from natural generation. A few named habitat/elevation
corrections and a redwood coastal envelope supplement country-scale native ranges.
These are approximate botanical-country ranges, not precise habitat maps.
Content models/textures/code remain supplied by the original mods; none is copied
into this data set. Exact input checksums, taxon IDs, exclusions and analogue rules
are recorded in the source repository at docs/research/tree-range-provenance.json
and tools/tree-taxa.json.
